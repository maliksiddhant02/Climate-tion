**How to use this library**

This library has four short parts and a glossary. **Part 1** explains what COP31 actually is and why hosting looks unusual this year. **Part 2** sets out the priority areas your solution should respond to. **Part 3** covers the Pacific and Australia's role in shaping COP31. **Part 4** looks at the EU as a global climate actor and its partnership with the Pacific. **COP31 in Context**

COP stands for **Conference of the Parties**: the annual meeting where countries that have signed the UN climate treaty negotiate how to act on it together. COP31 takes place in 2026, and **its hosting arrangement is** **the first of its kind.**

**Türkiye** is the formal host, holding COP31 and the World Leaders' Summit in Antalya. **Australia** takes on a separate role as President of Negotiations, leading the process of turning national positions into agreed text — an arrangement reached in partnership with **the Pacific** after Australia and Türkiye had both been competing to host the summit outright.

Before the formal summit, **Fiji hosts a Pre-COP** preparatory meeting and **Tuvalu hosts a Leaders' event**, both in the first week of October. These Pacific gatherings are designed to shape COP31's priorities in advance and put **Pacific-led solutions** in front of the world — and they follow directly on from this hackathon.

**The Priority Areas for Your Solution**

Türkiye's COP31 Presidency organises its Action Agenda around **ten priority themes**, each paired with a measurable target for 2035\. Climate Hack-tion asks teams to build a working solution addressing **one of five of them.**

| Electrification | Raise electricity's share of global final energy demand from just over 20% today to 35% by 2035 |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Waste & methane** | Halve the growth in global municipal solid waste by 2035 |
| **Resilient cities & buildings** | Cut energy-use intensity in the building sector by at least 25% by 2035 |
| **Green industrialisation** | Raise the global circular material-use rate to at least 15% by 2035 |
| **Climate awareness & education** | Work toward climate action education for all by 2035 |

These targets aren't hackathon inventions — they're the flagship goals the COP31 Presidency launched at the Bonn Climate Change Conference in June 2026, as part of a wider Action Agenda spanning ten themes in total, from oceans and food security to health systems. The five above are the ones Climate Hack-tion has chosen as tracks for teams to build against, so a strong project should be able to point to exactly which target it answers to.

**Australia and the Pacific**

Australia and Pacific partners are running **the COP31 Pacific Partnership** together, a joint effort to put the Blue Pacific — the region's shared identity and vision for its ocean and islands — at the centre of the world's attention, and to make sure Pacific voices and Pacific-led solutions shape the negotiations rather than sit on the sidelines of them.

That leadership runs through the region's own institutions, not just around them. **The Pacific Islands Forum Chair**, its Troika of past, current and incoming chairs, and the Forum Secretariat have facilitated the decision-making that led to Fiji and Tuvalu hosting the Pre-COP and Leaders' events — a process the Pacific itself steered.

Australia's specific role is narrower but significant: as President of Negotiations, it holds authority over the negotiating process itself — preparing draft texts, choosing co-facilitators and steering the summit's final “cover decision” — a role Australia's Minister for Climate Change and Energy carries out directly.

| :---- | :---- |
| **Regional framing** | The Blue Pacific |

**The EU and Global Climate Action**

At home, the EU has written its own climate ambition into law. **The European Climate Law** sets climate neutrality by 2050 as a binding goal, and since April 2026 it also sets a binding intermediate target: a 90% cut in net greenhouse gas emissions by 2040, compared with 1990 levels.

**The EU treats climate action as diplomacy as much as policy.** Its Joint Communication on the EU's global climate and energy vision, published in October 2025, frames the clean transition as central to Europe's competitiveness and security, and **commits the EU to using multilateral engagement to convince other countries to raise their own climate ambition**. The Council's most recent conclusions on EU energy and climate diplomacy, adopted in April 2026, go further: they explicitly name the threats rising sea levels pose to low-lying states and Small Island Developing States, and commit the EU to working with the UN on risks to their sovereignty and territorial integrity — a direct line to the Pacific region this hackathon is set in.

That commitment comes with a budget. In **2024, the EU and its 27 member states contributed €31.7 billion in climate finance from public sources**, and mobilised a further €11.0 billion in private finance, to help developing countries cut emissions and adapt to climate impacts. Together with the European Investment Bank, the EU describes itself as **the world's largest collective provider of public climate finance**.

In the Pacific specifically, that cooperation has a name and a budget of its own. **Global Gateway,** the EU's international investment strategy, runs a dedicated initiative for the region — **the Green Blue Alliance for the Pacifi**c — bringing together the EU, its member states, the European Investment Bank and 18 Pacific countries and territories around climate action, resilience and the sustainable use of natural capital. **Team Europe has committed a minimum of €650 million** to Global Gateway efforts **across the Pacific,** and announced close to €300 million in new investments and budget support at the first EU–Pacific Business Forum. The EU also holds regular **High-Level Dialogues on climate directly with Australia and New Zealand,** alongside its wider engagement across the Asia-Pacific region.

The day before hacking starts, the European Commission and the European Economic and Social Committee are co-hosting a Youth Policy Dialogue on COP31 in Brussels, with the European Youth Forum and Generation Climate Europe — a reminder that **this hackathon sits inside a much larger conversation about young people's place in climate diplomacy.**

**Key Terms**

**COP**

Conference of the Parties — the yearly meeting of countries party to the UN climate treaty (the UNFCCC).

**Paris Agreement**

The 2015 international treaty under which countries agreed to keep global warming well below 2°C, and pursue 1.5°C.

**NDC (Nationally Determined Contribution)**

A country's own climate action plan and emissions target under the Paris Agreement, updated roughly every five years.

**Article 6**

The part of the Paris Agreement that sets rules for countries trading emissions reductions between each other, including international carbon markets.

**Loss and Damage Fund**

A fund, agreed at COP28, to help countries already experiencing climate impacts — like sea-level rise or extreme storms — cope with harm that adaptation couldn't prevent.

**COP31 Action Agenda**

The set of non-negotiated initiatives, targets and partnerships a COP Presidency uses to drive action alongside the formal negotiations — including the ten priority themes and 2035 targets behind this hackathon's tracks.

**The Blue Pacific**

The Pacific Islands Forum's shared framing of the region as one ocean continent, used to present a unified Pacific voice in forums like COP31.

**European Climate Law   EU**

The EU regulation that makes climate neutrality by 2050, and an intermediate 90% emissions cut by 2040, legally binding across all member states.

*Source: [climate.ec.europa.eu](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/european-climate-law_en)*

**Global Gateway   EU**

The EU's strategy for financing infrastructure and sustainable development abroad, including the Green Blue Alliance for the Pacific.

*Source: [eeas.europa.eu](https://eeas.europa.eu/pacific)*

**Team Europe   EU**

The EU, its member states, and European financial institutions such as the European Investment Bank acting together on a shared initiative — the format behind the EU's Pacific climate commitments.

*Source: [eeas.europa.eu](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/climate-action_en)*

**International climate finance   EU**

Money richer countries provide or mobilise to help developing countries cut emissions and adapt to climate change. The EU and its member states contributed €31.7 billion in public climate finance in 2024, plus €11.0 billion in mobilised private finance.

*Source: [consilium.europa.eu](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/10/27/council-publishes-2024-international-climate-finance-figures/)*
